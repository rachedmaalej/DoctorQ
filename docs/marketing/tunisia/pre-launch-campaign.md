# BleSaf Pre-Launch Teaser Campaign
## Capturing the First 10 Founding Clinics in Tunis

**Status:** Draft — Open for Brainstorming  
**Last Updated:** February 2026

---

## 1. The Strategic Bet

We're not launching BleSaf with a generic product announcement. Instead, we're running a **3-beat drip campaign** designed to build anticipation, trigger word-of-mouth, and create a sense of exclusivity before anyone even sees the product.

The core insight: Tunis's medical community is tight-knit. Doctors share the same WhatsApp groups, attend the same conferences, and often work in the same clinic buildings. A well-crafted teaser doesn't just reach one doctor — it becomes a conversation topic among many. We're designing every communication to be **shareable and discussion-worthy**, not just informative.

Our goal is not to acquire hundreds of users. It's to hand-pick **10–20 founding clinics** who will use BleSaf for 3 months, help us fine-tune it for the Tunisian context, and become our most vocal advocates when we scale.

---

## 2. The 3-Beat Narrative Arc

Every great reveal follows a rhythm. Ours borrows from storytelling:

### Beat 1 — Empathy + Mystery (Day 0)
**Purpose:** Make the doctor feel seen. Surface the problem they've normalized. Offer zero solution — only a signal that *something* is coming.

- No product name, no logo, no features
- A provocative question or emotional statement about the waiting room experience
- Ends with a lightweight hook: a QR code, a "stay tuned," or a simple email capture
- **Designed to trigger:** "Did you get this too?" conversations

### Beat 2 — Partial Reveal + Social Proof (Day 4–5)
**Purpose:** Reward the curiosity from Beat 1. Offer a glimpse — not the full picture. Introduce social proof to create urgency.

- Reveal the product name (BleSaf) and a hint of what it does
- Show that other doctors are already interested ("7 cabinets ont confirmé")
- Include a visual tease: a blurred screenshot, a before/after, or a short video clip
- **Designed to trigger:** "I need to look into this before spots fill up"

### Beat 3 — Full Reveal + Exclusive Access (Day 8–10)
**Purpose:** Open the door. Present the landing page, the onboarding flow, and the "Founding Clinic" offer with all its advantages.

- Full landing page with interactive demo
- Clear value proposition: what BleSaf does, how it works, what it costs (free for founders)
- Application form for the Founding Clinic program
- **Designed to trigger:** Immediate signup driven by accumulated desire and scarcity

### Timing Rationale

The gaps between beats matter. Too short and it feels like spam. Too long and they forget. 4–5 days between each beat gives enough time for word-of-mouth to circulate while keeping momentum alive. The entire arc completes in under two weeks.

## 3. Five Storytelling Angles

We've identified five distinct emotional angles for the teaser content. Each targets a different psychological trigger. The final campaign may combine elements from several angles.

### Angle 1: "The Waiting Room Problem" — Lead with Patient Pain

Strip away all product talk. Instead, hold up a mirror to the doctor's blind spot: what their patients actually experience while waiting. This angle works because doctors are busy during consultations — they rarely witness the frustration building in the other room.

**Example Beat 1 message (physical card):**

> *"Vos patients attendent en moyenne 47 minutes avant de vous voir. Ils ne vous le disent pas, mais ils y pensent."*
>
> *(Your patients wait an average of 47 minutes before seeing you. They don't tell you, but they think about it.)*

No logo. No branding. Just a QR code leading to a page that says: *"Un changement arrive. Laissez votre email pour être parmi les premiers."*

**Why it works:** It's uncomfortable in a productive way. There's nothing to buy, nothing to dismiss — just a truth they can't unsee. And because it's mysterious, they'll mention it to colleagues.

### Angle 2: "The Before/After" — Visual Contrast

Use imagery that every Tunisian doctor recognizes viscerally: the crowded, chaotic waiting room. Then, in Beat 2, show the same space transformed — calm patients, spaced out, checking their phones because they know exactly when their turn is.

**Example Beat 1 message (WhatsApp or social):**

A photo of a packed Tunisian clinic waiting room with the caption:

> *"Chaque jour, la même scène. Et si ça pouvait changer en 5 minutes?"*
>
> *(Every day, the same scene. What if it could change in 5 minutes?)*

**Example Beat 2 follow-up:**

Same visual angle, but patients are calm and few. Overlay text:

> *"Ils savent exactement quand c'est leur tour. Découvrez comment bientôt."*
>
> *(They know exactly when it's their turn. Find out how soon.)*

**Why it works:** The brain processes images 60,000× faster than text. The contrast does all the selling without a single feature bullet point. Highly shareable on WhatsApp.

### Angle 3: "The Exclusive Club" — Status and Scarcity

Frame early access as an invitation to a founding circle, not a beta test. Tunisian doctors respond to professional status — being "selected" rather than "marketed to" changes the entire power dynamic.

**Example Beat 1 message (premium printed card, hand-delivered):**

> *"Docteur [Nom], vous avez été sélectionné(e) pour participer à quelque chose de nouveau dans la médecine tunisienne."*
>
> *(Doctor [Name], you have been selected to participate in something new in Tunisian medicine.)*
>
> *— L'équipe BleSaf. Plus de détails suivront.*

**Example Beat 2 message (WhatsApp):**

> *"7 cabinets ont déjà confirmé leur participation au programme fondateur BleSaf. Il reste 3 places. Voici un premier aperçu..."* [blurred dashboard screenshot]

**Why it works:** Scarcity is real (we genuinely only want 10–20 clinics). The invitation format elevates the interaction from a sales pitch to a professional opportunity. The counter in Beat 2 creates authentic urgency.

### Angle 4: "The Patient's Voice" — Testimonial-First

Let patients speak. Use anonymous quotes (real or realistic) that surface the emotional cost of waiting. No doctor wants to be the one whose patients are leaving without being seen.

**Example Beat 1 (clean card with 3 quotes):**

> *"J'ai attendu 2 heures sans savoir si le docteur m'a oublié."*
>
> *"Je suis venue avec mon bébé. Il n'y avait même pas de place pour s'asseoir."*
>
> *"J'ai quitté sans voir le docteur. C'était la troisième fois."*
>
> Below: *"Vos patients parlent. Bientôt, vous aurez une réponse."*

**Example Beat 2 (the flip — same format, positive quotes):**

> *"Je savais exactement quand c'était mon tour. J'ai attendu au café d'en face."*
>
> *"Le docteur m'a appelé par notification. Pas besoin de rester dans la salle d'attente."*
>
> Below: *"Ce n'est pas un rêve. C'est BleSaf. Détails dans 3 jours."*

**Why it works:** The escalation across the three negative quotes (mild frustration → hardship → abandonment) builds emotional pressure. The flip in Beat 2 provides relief through the same format, making the solution feel tangible through human stories.

### Angle 5: "The Numbers Don't Lie" — Data-Driven

Quantify the invisible cost. Reframe queue chaos from an annoyance into a revenue problem. Doctors are scientists — numbers resonate more than emotional pitches for many of them.

**Example Beat 1 message:**

> *"Un cabinet perd en moyenne 3 patients par semaine à cause de l'attente. Ça fait 150 patients par an. Ça fait [X] dinars de chiffre d'affaires perdu."*
>
> *(A clinic loses an average of 3 patients per week due to wait times. That's 150 patients per year. That's [X] dinars in lost revenue.)*

**Example Beat 2 message:**

> *"Les cabinets qui digitalisent leur file d'attente réduisent les départs de 60% et augmentent la satisfaction patient de 40%. Quelque chose arrive pour les cabinets tunisiens."*

**Why it works:** The revenue framing makes BleSaf a business investment, not just a convenience tool. When you show a doctor they're losing 150 patients a year, the subscription price becomes trivial in comparison.

## 4. Channel Strategy

The medium shapes the message. Here's how we see each channel fitting the campaign:

### Physical Cards (Beat 1 — Primary)
Hand-delivered to 30–50 target clinics in Tunis. This is the highest-impact channel because it signals seriousness, it's tactile, and it can't be swiped away or buried in a notification feed. The card itself becomes a talking point. Cost is low (printing + a day of delivery), impact is disproportionately high.

### WhatsApp (Beat 2 — Primary)
Personal, high open rates, and — critically — easy to forward. If we capture phone numbers or emails from the Beat 1 QR code, Beat 2 goes directly to interested doctors. WhatsApp is also where Tunisian doctors already discuss professional topics informally. A well-crafted message here gets forwarded to group chats organically.

### Facebook/Instagram (Supporting — All Beats)
Targeted ads reaching Tunisian healthcare professionals. Useful for broader awareness and for retargeting people who engaged with Beat 1 content. Less personal than WhatsApp or physical cards, but extends reach beyond our hand-delivered list.

### Doctor WhatsApp Groups (Organic — High Impact)
If we can get even one engaged doctor to share the teaser in a professional WhatsApp group, the reach multiplies dramatically. This isn't something we control directly — but every piece of content should be designed with the question: *"Would a doctor screenshot this and share it?"*

## 5. The "Clinique Fondatrice" Incentive Package

The founding clinic offer needs to feel genuinely valuable — not like a discount code. These doctors are giving us something precious (their time, their patience with bugs, their professional reputation by association). The package should reflect that.

**Ideas currently on the table:**

- **"Clinique Fondatrice" badge** — permanently displayed in-app and on their patient-facing page. This is a status symbol, not just a label.
- **Free or deeply discounted access** — free on a basic tier for life, or a significant lifetime discount on premium.
- **Priority feature requests** — their feedback directly shapes the roadmap. They're not just users; they're co-builders.
- **Referral revenue share** — a meaningful commission for every clinic they bring in during the first year.
- **Co-branding opportunity** — featured as case studies and testimonials on the BleSaf website and marketing materials, with their permission.
- **Early access to every new feature** — they see it first, before general release.
- **Quarterly "Founding Circle" dinner** — this is deeply Tunisian culturally. Shared meals build loyalty and community in ways that emails never will.

## 6. Open Questions for Brainstorming

This strategy has a strong foundation but several critical decisions remain open. These are the areas where additional thinking, debate, and creative input will have the most impact.

### On Targeting and Selection

- **How do we select the initial 30–50 clinics for physical card delivery?** Should we focus on a specific neighborhood in Tunis, a specific medical specialty, or a mix? Ophthalmology clinics (given DoctorQ's origins) might be the easiest first vertical, but general practitioners have higher volume.
- **Should we target younger doctors or established ones?** Younger doctors are more tech-savvy and building their practice (more motivated to improve patient experience). Established doctors have larger patient volumes and more credibility for word-of-mouth. Which matters more at this stage?
- **Do we have any existing connections in the medical community?** A warm introduction from a respected doctor would dramatically amplify every beat of the campaign. Even one advocate changes the dynamic.

### On Messaging and Tone

- **How far can we push the "provocative" angle?** The waiting room mirror (Angle 1) is intentionally uncomfortable. Is there a risk of alienating doctors who feel accused rather than understood? Should we soften it or lean in?
- **Should the campaign be in French, Tunisian Arabic (Derja), or a mix?** French carries professional authority. Derja carries warmth and authenticity. A code-switching approach (French for formal beats, Derja for WhatsApp) might work best, but needs validation.
- **Can we source real patient quotes?** Authentic testimonials from Tunisian patients would be far more powerful than fabricated ones. Could we run a quick informal survey at a few clinics? Even 10 real quotes would transform Angle 4.

### On the Founding Clinic Program

- **What's the right number of founding clinics?** 10 is intimate and manageable. 20 gives more data but more support burden. Is there a sweet spot?
- **What commitment do we ask from founders?** Three months of active use is the minimum for meaningful feedback. But should we formalize it? A light agreement? A WhatsApp group check-in cadence?
- **How do we handle it if a founding clinic has a bad experience?** Bugs are inevitable. Response time to founder issues needs to be exceptional — same-day at minimum. Do we assign a dedicated point of contact for each clinic?
- **What happens after the 3-month founding period?** Do they transition to a paid plan with a discount? Stay free forever? The answer shapes how we position the offer.

### On Execution and Timing

- **What's our actual timeline?** When is the product ready enough to survive real clinic usage? The teaser campaign should start 2–3 weeks before that date, which means we need to work backward from product readiness.
- **Who delivers the physical cards?** This person is the first human touchpoint for BleSaf. Their appearance, demeanor, and ability to answer basic questions matters. Is it Rached personally? A hired professional? A medical student?
- **Should Beat 3 include a video?** A 60-second walkthrough video showing a patient checking in and a doctor managing the queue could be more persuasive than any landing page. But it requires production time.
- **How do we measure success at each beat?** Beat 1: QR code scans and email captures. Beat 2: WhatsApp response rate and forward rate. Beat 3: Founding clinic applications. What are our target numbers?

### On Combining Angles

- **Which angles should we layer for maximum impact?** Our current thinking is: Angle 1 (Pain) + Angle 3 (Exclusivity) for Beat 1, Angle 2 (Before/After) + Angle 5 (Numbers) for Beat 2. But this is open for debate.
- **Should different doctor segments receive different angles?** Perhaps younger doctors respond better to Angle 4 (Patient Voice) while established ones respond to Angle 5 (Numbers). Is segment-specific messaging worth the added complexity for a 30–50 person campaign?
- **Is there a sixth angle we haven't considered?** Peer competition ("Dr. X down the street is already digitizing — are you?"), technology leadership ("Be the first in your arrondissement"), or even humor could work. What else?
